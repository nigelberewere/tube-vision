# CTA Templates by Channel Type and Tone

The CTA is the last 30 seconds of the video. It should feel like a natural continuation of the
content — not a gear-shift into sales mode. The moment it feels like an ask, you've lost them.

**Universal rule:** One ask per video. Subscribe, comment, or watch next — never all three.

---

## CTA by tone

### Dark / Serious / Documentary
Frame the subscribe as access to a rare category of content. Never thank the viewer for watching —
it implies you're surprised they stayed.

**Template:**
> "[One sentence that crystallises the video's core insight.] That's not [the official narrative /
> ancient history / a coincidence]. That's how [power / systems / people] actually work.
>
> If you want more of the stories that didn't make the curriculum — you know where to find me.
> I'll see you next time."

**Comment prompt option:**
> "What's the [smallest decision / one moment / person] you think changed the most that nobody
> talks about? Drop it below."

---

### Educational / Explanatory
Frame the subscribe as an ongoing learning relationship. Warm but not sycophantic.

**Template:**
> "The [concept] isn't complicated once you see it this way. It's just that almost nobody
> explains it like this.
>
> If this helped — there's a lot more where that came from. Hit subscribe and I'll see you in
> the next one."

**Comment prompt option:**
> "What part of this surprised you most? I'm genuinely curious — let me know below."

---

### Commentary / Opinion
Frame the subscribe as ongoing access to a perspective they won't get elsewhere.

**Template:**
> "You don't have to agree with me. But I'd rather have this conversation than not have it.
>
> If you want more of this — subscribe. And if you think I got something wrong, tell me in
> the comments. I actually read them."

**Comment prompt option:**
> "Where do you land on this? I want to see the range of opinions — drop it below."

---

### Finance / Self-improvement
Frame the subscribe as an ongoing competitive advantage. The viewer gains something practical
by staying.

**Template:**
> "Most people will never know this. Which is actually the point — the people who do know it
> act on it, and the gap widens.
>
> Subscribe if you want to keep closing that gap. New video every [cadence]."

**Comment prompt option:**
> "Which of these are you going to implement first? Tell me below — I'll follow up in a future video."

---

### True Crime / Mystery
Frame the comment section as an extension of the investigation. The viewer has something to contribute.

**Template:**
> "[Unresolved element of the case]. That question has never been answered to my satisfaction.
> Maybe it has been to yours.
>
> Tell me what you think happened. I read every comment, and I've changed my mind more than
> once because of what someone said below."

**Comment prompt option:**
> "What's the detail that bothers you most about this case? Let's work through it together."

---

## End card strategy

The end card video should be selected by **thematic proximity**, not recency.

| Video type | Best end card pairing |
|---|---|
| "How X caused Y" | Another causation story in the same era/niche |
| True crime: unsolved | Another unsolved case (drives binge-watching) |
| "The hidden history of X" | "The hidden history of Y" — builds series habit |
| Finance framework | The most common mistake in that same domain |
| Opinion / commentary | The steelman of the opposing view |

**Never queue:** Your most recent video if it's unrelated. The end card is not a newsletter —
it's a recommendation. Treat it like one.

---

## What to never say in a CTA

- "If you enjoyed this video..." — conditional, implies they might not have
- "Smash that like button" — 2016 called
- "Don't forget to subscribe" — implies they would forget, which implies the content wasn't memorable
- "It really helps the channel" — guilt-based ask; treats the viewer as a donor, not an audience
- "I'll see you in the next video" as your only CTA — missed opportunity; always pair with one ask
