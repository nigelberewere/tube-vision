# Hook Formulas by Niche

The hook is the most important 30–50 seconds of any YouTube video. A bad hook kills a great video.
A great hook saves a mediocre one. Write the hook last — after you know what the video's strongest
moment is — then engineer the hook to make that moment feel inevitable.

---

## The 6 hook formulas

### 1. The Destabilising Fact
State something true that the viewer doesn't know — and that immediately makes them question
something they thought they understood.

> "In 1983, a Soviet lieutenant colonel received a warning that the United States had just launched
> five nuclear missiles toward Moscow. He had four minutes to decide whether to retaliate.
> He chose not to report it. He was right. And no one found out for fifteen years."

**Works for:** History, true crime, science, finance
**Avoid if:** Your channel is warm/personal — feels too cold for lifestyle or self-improvement niches

---

### 2. The Contradiction Hook
Open with two facts that shouldn't both be true. The brain is wired to resolve contradictions —
the viewer stays to watch the resolution.

> "France declared war on Prussia in 1870. France lost. And somehow — the man who engineered that
> defeat was French."

**Works for:** History, geopolitics, commentary, finance
**Avoid if:** Your contradiction is too obscure — the viewer needs to care about at least one side of it

---

### 3. The Outcome First (In Media Res)
Start with the ending — then pull back to show how we got there. Creates instant investment.

> "By the time the phone call ended, the company was worth nothing. The call had lasted eleven minutes.
> This is how it happened."

**Works for:** True crime, business stories, personal narratives, documentary
**Avoid if:** The ending is so dramatic it feels unbelievable — you'll lose them before you earn the trust

---

### 4. The Pain Hook
Name the exact problem the viewer is currently experiencing. Creates instant recognition and relief
("finally, someone is talking about this").

> "You've been doing everything right. Eating well. Sleeping eight hours. Still exhausted.
> Nobody told you about the one thing that actually causes this."

**Works for:** Health, fitness, finance, self-improvement, productivity
**Avoid if:** The pain is too niche — you'll only hook the narrow slice who have that exact problem

---

### 5. The Forbidden Knowledge Hook
Imply that information exists that someone, somewhere, didn't want the viewer to have.

> "This story was in the historical record the entire time. It wasn't hidden. It was just never taught.
> And once you know why — that becomes the most interesting part."

**Works for:** History, investigative content, conspiracy-adjacent topics (handled responsibly)
**Avoid if:** Your evidence doesn't actually support the implication — this hook requires delivery

---

### 6. The Scale Hook
Open with a number so large, or a consequence so vast, that it demands explanation.

> "One memo. Written on a Tuesday afternoon in 1953. It set in motion a chain of events that killed
> an estimated two million people over the next twenty years."

**Works for:** History, geopolitics, economics, true crime
**Avoid if:** Your video can't actually connect the dots — the hook must be paid off

---

## Hook don'ts (common mistakes that kill retention)

- **"Hey guys, welcome back to the channel."** Never. This is a gift to the skip button.
- **"In this video, I'm going to show you..."** Announcing the video instead of starting it.
- **Slow music + slow zoom for 10 seconds.** The viewer has already left.
- **"Before we get into it, make sure to subscribe."** This signals that the video itself is not good enough to earn the subscribe.
- **Opening with credentials.** "As someone who has studied this for years..." Nobody cares yet. Earn the credibility through the content.
- **Saving the best for last.** The best thing in the video should be teased in the hook, not saved. Viewers don't wait anymore.

## Hook length by format

| Format | Hook length | Word count |
|---|---|---|
| Short (3–6 min) | 20–30 seconds | 60–80 words |
| Mid (8–14 min) | 40–60 seconds | 100–150 words |
| Long (16–22 min) | 60–90 seconds | 150–220 words |
| Shorts (under 60 sec) | 3 seconds | 10–15 words |

---

## Hook checklist

Before finalising the hook, verify:
- [ ] Does it open a loop the viewer will stay to close?
- [ ] Does it work without music (first 15 seconds)?
- [ ] Does it avoid announcing what the video will cover?
- [ ] Is the first sentence under 12 words?
- [ ] Does it feel like the video has already started — not like an intro?
- [ ] Will it make sense to someone who has never heard of this channel?
